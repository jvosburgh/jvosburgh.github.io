# GoVAC® MAX System

**Narrative Type:** Product Positioning & Market Creation Narrative

## Overview

The GoVAC® MAX narrative was developed to support the commercialization of a new methane capture and recovery platform for transmission pipeline operators. The challenge was not simply introducing another piece of equipment to the market—it was reframing how operators thought about pipeline blowdowns altogether. Through market analysis, customer research, regulatory trends, emissions data, and product differentiation, I developed a narrative that repositioned pipeline evacuation from a routine maintenance activity into a strategic emissions reduction opportunity with measurable operational, environmental, and financial value.

## Challenge

Pipeline operators have historically relied on venting or flaring during maintenance activities such as integrity work, equipment replacement, pressure reductions, emergency response, and pigging operations. While accepted practice, these activities release methane emissions, waste saleable natural gas, and create growing compliance challenges as environmental regulations continue to evolve.

At the same time, customers often viewed pipeline evacuation as an unavoidable operational expense rather than an opportunity for methane reduction, product recovery, or operational improvement. Existing market conversations focused primarily on technical specifications and evacuation processes, making it difficult to articulate the broader strategic value of capture and recovery technology.

The challenge was to establish a compelling market narrative that connected changing industry priorities—including emissions reduction, regulatory compliance, ESG commitments, operational efficiency, and product recovery—to a solution that operators could realistically deploy within existing workflows.

## Insight

My research uncovered a critical market truth:

**Pipeline blowdowns represent one of the largest overlooked methane reduction opportunities in the natural gas industry.**

Analysis of regulatory reporting, emissions data, pipeline infrastructure metrics, and operator maintenance practices revealed that tens of thousands of evacuation projects occur annually across North America. More importantly, the majority of emissions associated with those activities fall within the operating profile of portable methane capture and recovery technology.

The research also revealed that pipeline pigging operations represented a substantial and often underreported source of methane emissions, significantly expanding the addressable opportunity beyond traditional maintenance projects.

Customer conversations surfaced another important insight: operators needed more than an emissions solution. They wanted equipment that was easy to deploy, minimized operational complexity, reduced the need for additional resources, generated defensible environmental reporting, and fit seamlessly into existing field operations.

## Narrative

For decades, pipeline operators accepted methane venting as an unavoidable part of maintaining safe and reliable natural gas infrastructure.

Every maintenance project, integrity program, equipment replacement, pressure reduction, or pigging operation required sections of pipeline to be evacuated. The result was often the same: valuable natural gas released into the atmosphere through venting or flaring.

As methane regulations intensified and environmental performance became a higher strategic priority, operators found themselves under pressure to reduce emissions without sacrificing operational efficiency or introducing additional complexity into field operations.

The challenge wasn't a lack of awareness.

The challenge was a lack of practical solutions.

Many alternatives required external power, larger equipment footprints, additional support resources, or operational tradeoffs that limited scalability across a pipeline network.

GoVAC® MAX was positioned to eliminate those tradeoffs.

Built on patented compression technology, the system powers itself using natural gas already present in the pipeline. This eliminates the need for external power sources, reduces operational complexity, and minimizes the overall emissions profile of evacuation projects. Its compact footprint and towable design allow operators to deploy the system in space-constrained locations and mobilize it using a standard pickup truck, making it practical for real-world field operations.

The system's high-flow performance allows operators to rapidly evacuate larger transmission pipeline projects while drawing pipelines down to near-zero pressure and transferring gas into adjacent pipeline systems. Instead of venting methane into the atmosphere, operators recover and preserve product value.

However, the differentiated value of GoVAC MAX extends beyond compression performance.

As environmental reporting and regulatory accountability become increasingly important, operators need trusted data to validate emissions reductions and demonstrate compliance. Through automated controls, remote monitoring, secure data management, and validated environmental reporting, GoVAC MAX provides operators with documented proof of environmental and operational performance, creating confidence in emissions reporting and sustainability initiatives.

This shifted the market conversation away from equipment specifications and toward business outcomes.

GoVAC MAX was no longer positioned as a compressor.

It was positioned as a self-contained methane capture and recovery platform that helps operators recover product, reduce emissions, simplify field operations, support regulatory compliance, and generate verified environmental results from activities that had historically been viewed as unavoidable sources of methane emissions.

Backed by data demonstrating approximately 48,000 annual projects and more than 430,000 metric tons of addressable methane emissions across the United States and Canada, the narrative established GoVAC MAX as a strategic decarbonization solution for transmission pipeline operators rather than a specialized maintenance tool.

## Activation

The narrative became the foundation for:

- Product positioning and messaging strategy
- Product launch and commercialization planning
- Website and product collateral development
- Market segmentation between GoVAC FLEX and GoVAC MAX
- Sales enablement materials
- Customer presentations and business case development
- Executive and investor presentations
- Regulatory and sustainability communications
- Market opportunity and growth planning

## Impact

- Established a quantified North American market opportunity of approximately 94,000 annual pipeline evacuation projects.
- Defined a serviceable market of roughly 48,000 projects well suited for GoVAC MAX.
- Quantified more than 430,000 metric tons of addressable methane emissions associated with those projects.
- Created a repeatable business case connecting methane reduction, recovered gas value, operational efficiency, and regulatory compliance.
- Aligned product, commercial, executive, and investor stakeholders around a common market narrative.
- Repositioned GoVAC MAX from a field service tool to a strategic emissions reduction platform.
- Provided the foundation for commercialization, customer engagement, and long-term growth strategy.

## Why This Example Matters

The value was not simply sizing a market. It was uncovering a market truth, defining the customer problem behind the data, translating technical differentiation into customer value, and developing a narrative that changed how customers, stakeholders, and investors understood both the problem and the role of the product in solving it.

Rather than leading with technology, the narrative led with a simple insight:

**Methane emissions from routine pipeline operations are recoverable—and GoVAC MAX provides operators with a practical way to capture that value.**
