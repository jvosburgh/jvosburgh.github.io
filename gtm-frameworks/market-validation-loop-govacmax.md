# Market Validation Loop: GoVAC™ MAX Market Opportunity
**Author:** Jason Vosburgh  
**Last Updated:** August 2022

---

## Overview
This Market Validation Loop documents a repeatable, evidence‑based process used to validate the national market size for pipeline blowdowns serviceable by GoVAC™ MAX. Rather than producing a one‑time research report, this loop demonstrates how structured hypotheses, signal models, discovery cycles, and cross‑dataset validation were used to refine and confirm the market opportunity.

The loop reflects the full validation process applied to SB1371 compliance data, EPA FLIGHT emissions data, PHMSA pipeline mileage, and PRCI industry research.

---

## 1. Market Hypothesis
The initial hypothesis guiding this loop:

- Pipeline blowdowns are frequent, recurring operational events across U.S. transmission and distribution systems.
- A significant portion of these blowdowns are large enough to require MAX‑class recompression technology.
- California’s SB1371 reporting can serve as a reliable proxy for national blowdown frequency when normalized.
- The total annual blowdown volume is large enough to justify investment in GoVAC MAX.

This hypothesis is intentionally falsifiable. Each loop tests whether operational data supports or contradicts these assumptions.

---

## 2. Validation Signals
Signals used to evaluate the hypothesis:

### Operational Frequency Signals
- Miles‑per‑blowdown ratios for distribution and transmission pipelines.
- Year‑over‑year changes in blowdown frequency.

### Market Size Signals
- Total U.S. pipeline miles × validated blowdown factors.
- Annual blowdown counts derived from multiple datasets.

### Technical Applicability Signals
- Identification of blowdowns too small for GoVAC technology.
- Segmentation of FLEX‑class vs MAX‑class serviceable events.

### Data Reliability Signals
- Consistency across SB1371, EPA FLIGHT, PHMSA, and PRCI datasets.
- Detection of anomalies or reporting inconsistencies.

These signals form the backbone of the validation model.

---

## 3. Discovery Loop
Each cycle of the loop follows the same structure:

### Step 1: Extract and Normalize Data
- SB1371 compliance reports (2020 → 2021) analyzed for blowdown counts and pipeline miles.
- Utility reporting differences normalized for comparability.

### Step 2: Identify Anomalies
- Southern California Gas Company’s 2021 transmission blowdown count included estimated venting events too small for GoVAC applicability.
- These events were removed to avoid inflating the TAM.
- Joint reporting between SoCalGas and SDG&E required interpretation adjustments.

### Step 3: Recalculate Blowdown Factors
- Distribution and transmission miles‑per‑blowdown ratios recalculated for each year.
- Year‑over‑year shifts analyzed to understand variance.

### Step 4: Synthesize Learnings
- Updated factors applied to national pipeline mileage to estimate annual blowdowns.
- Findings compared against independent datasets.

This loop was repeated as new data became available.

---

## 4. Problem Validation
Not all blowdowns represent real market opportunity. This stage validates which operational events are serviceable by GoVAC MAX.

### Key Activities
- Removal of blowdowns too small for recompression technology.
- Segmentation of events into FLEX‑class and MAX‑class categories.
- Validation of gas volumes using EPA FLIGHT emissions data.
- Review of PRCI blowdown categorization to understand event types and operational drivers.

This ensures the TAM reflects serviceable events, not just reported occurrences.

---

## 5. Cross‑Dataset Validation
Independent sources used to validate the market:

### SB1371 (California Regulatory Data)
- Detailed blowdown counts and pipeline miles.
- Useful for establishing baseline factors.

### EPA FLIGHT (National Emissions Reporting)
- Blowdown counts, methane emissions, and CO2e conversions.
- Validates gas volumes and event scale.

### PHMSA (National Pipeline Mileage)
- Authoritative pipeline mileage for transmission systems.
- Used to extrapolate national blowdown counts.

### PRCI (Industry Research)
- Trend analysis and blowdown categorization.
- Validates operational patterns and reporting consistency.

Cross‑validation strengthens confidence in the TAM.

---

## 6. Synthesis of Findings

### 2020 SB1371‑Based Estimate
- Distribution blowdowns: 39,367  
- Transmission blowdowns: 44,343  
- **Total:** 83,710 annual blowdowns

### 2021 SB1371‑Based Estimate
- Distribution blowdowns: 36,475  
- Transmission blowdowns: 17,111  
- **Total:** 53,586 annual blowdowns

### EPA FLIGHT‑Based Estimate (Transmission Only)
- Transmission blowdowns: ~19,016  
- Associated emissions: ~204,920 MT CH4  
- Miles‑per‑blowdown factor: 14.88

### Interpretation of Variance
The ~30,000 difference between 2020 and 2021 SB1371 estimates is driven by:

- Removal of small, non‑serviceable venting events in SoCalGas reporting.
- Joint reporting between SoCalGas and SDG&E requiring adjusted assumptions.
- Confidentiality of PG&E’s 2021 distribution data, requiring reuse of 2020 factors.

These adjustments reflect the importance of interpretation rules within the validation loop.

---

## 7. Decision
Based on the synthesized evidence:

- The market for pipeline blowdowns is real, recurring, and quantifiable.
- The MAX‑class opportunity is smaller than FLEX but significant at national scale.
- Independent datasets validate the overall magnitude of the market.
- Interpretation rules must be standardized for future loops.
- Further validation loops should incorporate utility‑specific operational practices to refine segmentation accuracy.

The market hypothesis is validated. The GoVAC MAX opportunity warrants continued investment and refinement.
